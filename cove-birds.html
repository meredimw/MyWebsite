Cove Birds

A simple catalog of birds spotted around Murden Cove — photo first, then the facts.

Red-necked Grebe

Podiceps grisegena
Waterbird Grebe Winter coastal visitor

Quick facts

ID keys Long-bodied grebe; pale cheeks; in breeding plumage, a reddish neck; often shows a yellowish bill.
Size 18–20 in (46–51 cm) in length.
Where to look (habitat) Coasts/shorelines and wetlands; in winter, often on salt water including protected bays and sometimes offshore.
Behavior Strong swimmer; direct flight with rapid wingbeats; can be clumsy taking off.
Conservation status IUCN: Least Concern.
Murden Cove note: Grebes ride low and dive like they’re teleporting. If you lose one, scan 30–80 yards out — they often surface much farther away than you expect.
Source: Audubon Field Guide page for Red-necked Grebe Open Audubon Guide

Great Blue Heron

Ardea herodias
Wader Shoreline hunter Year-round local

Quick facts

ID keys Tall gray-blue heron with an S-curved neck; dagger-like yellow bill; slow, powerful wingbeats in flight with neck tucked.
Size About 4 ft tall; wingspan roughly 6+ ft — the “pterodactyl of the cove” when it lifts off.
Where to look (habitat) Shallow shoreline edges, docks, tidal flats, and creek mouths — anywhere fish get pinned in skinny water.
Feeding style Stand-still ambush hunter — slow steps, long pause, then a lightning spear-stab for fish/crabs.
Common confusion Not a crane: herons fly with their neck tucked; cranes fly with neck extended.
Murden Cove note: The heron is the cove’s statue that moves. You’ll see one posted up on a rock or dock like it’s been there all morning — and it probably has. If you drift quietly, you can watch the whole hunt: micro-steps, freeze, head tilt to judge refraction, then the strike.
Reference: Great Blue Heron overview Open Audubon Guide
